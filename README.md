# Australian Family Law Self-Help Skills

A set of Claude "skills" that help self-represented people in Australian family law matters check their own documents against the law: compliance with court orders and child support agreements, the conduct of lawyers and experts, and how to write a complaint to a regulator.

## How to use

Start with `GETTING-STARTED.md`. Each skill folder also has a `USER-GUIDE.md`.

## Skills

| Skill | What it does |
|---|---|
| `family-orders-compliance-check` | Checks compliance with final parenting orders, property orders and a Binding Child Support Agreement |
| `single-expert-report-review` | Reviews a single expert report (Part 7.1) or a court report such as a Child Impact Report or Family Report (s 62G) |
| `icl-transcript-review` | Reviews an Independent Children's Lawyer's conduct |
| `party-solicitor-conduct-review` | Reviews a solicitor's conduct for a regulator or costs complaint |
| `complaint-letter-format` | A format for complaints and submissions to regulators |

## Using another AI (ChatGPT, Gemini, Grok and others)

These skills were written for Claude, so some instructions name Claude-only features. Before you rely on a skill in another AI, upload the skill file and ask:

> Review this skill. Rewrite any instruction that depends on a tool or feature you do not have, so it works with your own tools. Do not change the legal content, the checklists or the citations. List every change you made.

Then read the list of changes. If the AI altered a legal test, a section number or a deadline, discard that change. Results may differ between AI tools, and none of them replace legal advice.

## Privacy

Do not put real names, children's details, case numbers or sealed documents into a public repository or any public tool. Section 114Q of the Family Law Act 1975 (Cth) restricts publishing material that identifies people involved in proceedings.
