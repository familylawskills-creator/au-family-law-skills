# User guide: Party Solicitor Conduct Review

**Skill folder:** `party-solicitor-conduct-review`

## What it does
Reviews a solicitor's conduct in FCFCOA proceedings against the Legal Profession Uniform Law (NSW), the Australian Solicitors' Conduct Rules 2015, the Family Law Act and the FCFCOA Rules. It works for your own solicitor, a former solicitor or the other side's solicitor, and says whether each breach supports a court, disciplinary or civil consequence.

## When to use it
Use it for a complaint to the OLSC, Law Society or Legal Aid, a costs dispute, or to decide whether a solicitor's conduct is worth raising. Not for an ICL or an expert (use those skills).

**Example requests:**
- "Review my former solicitor's costs disclosure and bills."
- "Did the other side's solicitor mislead the court in this transcript?"
- "Is this conduct worth an OLSC complaint?"

## What to give it
Upload or paste these. The first one is required; the rest make the result stronger.

1. **A primary source** (required): transcript, judgment, filed documents, correspondence, costs agreement, bills, trust statements or file notes.
2. **Whose lawyer** they were: yours now, yours before, or the other party's.
3. Whether they were Legal Aid or private, and whether a barrister was briefed.

If something is missing, the skill marks the affected checks "Not assessable" and asks for it. It asks one question at a time.

## What you get back
- A report: matter context, summary verdict per area, a findings table with a consequence column, the strongest findings with quotes and seriousness, gaps and next steps, and sources.

## How to read the results
**Your own solicitor** owes you duties of competence, advice, costs disclosure and trust money handling. **The other side's solicitor** owes you only the duties to the court and to opponents (rules 19 to 35). The report applies only the duties that fit.

Seriousness: a single lapse is usually "unsatisfactory professional conduct", not "professional misconduct".

## Tips
- Dates matter for costs: disclosure must come before the work.
- You can ask for your own file from a former solicitor; the report tells you how.
- Raising an allegation without a proper basis can itself breach the rules, so let the evidence lead.

## Works with
- `complaint-letter-format`: to turn findings into a complaint
- `icl-transcript-review`: for the ICL

## Before you rely on it
- **Not legal advice.** It checks documents against the law. It does not tell you what to do in your case. Speak to a solicitor, Legal Aid or LawAccess NSW before acting.
- **Check every citation.** The skill tells the AI to verify current law, but AI can still be wrong. Open each section and case it cites.
- **Keep it private.** Section 114Q of the Family Law Act 1975 (Cth) makes it an offence to publish anything that identifies people in family law proceedings. Never post a report online or share it outside your lawyer, the court or a regulator.
