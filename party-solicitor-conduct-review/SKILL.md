# Party solicitor conduct review (FCFCOA, NSW practitioner)

Assess whether a solicitor acting for a party in FCFCOA proceedings complied with the state rules that govern the profession and the federal rules that govern the proceedings, and produce a report in which every adverse finding is pinned to an exact passage of a source document and an exact provision. The output must survive being handed to the Legal Services Commissioner, a Law Society complaints solicitor, a costs assessor or a judge, so precision and proportion matter more than volume. A solicitor who complied is recorded as compliant; over-reach destroys the credibility of the genuine findings.

## Why the review is two-sided

A family law solicitor in NSW answers to two regulators at once, and the review must keep them apart because the complaint pathways differ:

1. **State: the profession.** The Legal Profession Uniform Law (NSW) (applied by the Legal Profession Uniform Law Application Act 2014 (NSW)), the Legal Profession Uniform General Rules 2015 and the Legal Profession Uniform Law Australian Solicitors' Conduct Rules 2015 (ASCR). These govern costs disclosure, trust money, conflicts, confidentiality, competence, honesty and the duty to the court. A breach here is **unsatisfactory professional conduct** (section 296) or **professional misconduct** (section 297) and goes to the OLSC.
2. **Federal: the proceedings.** The Family Law Act 1975 (Cth), the Federal Circuit and Family Court of Australia Act 2021 (Cth) and the FCFCOA (Family Law) Rules 2021. These govern the overarching purpose, the lawyer's duty to assist the court, pre-action procedures, full and frank disclosure, service, undertakings, the implied (Harman) undertaking, family violence protocols and information the lawyer must give the client. A breach here is dealt with by the court (costs against the lawyer, orders), and only becomes a disciplinary matter where it also breaches the ASCR.

Three further distinctions shape every finding:

- **Whose lawyer.** A solicitor owes the full retainer duties (competence, instructions, advice, costs disclosure, confidentiality) only to their own client. To the other party they owe the duties in ASCR rules 19 to 35 (court, opponents, other persons) and nothing more. The user can complain about the other side's solicitor only on the second set.
- **Solicitor or counsel.** In-court conduct is the conduct of whoever was on their feet. Counsel is bound by the Legal Profession Uniform Conduct (Barristers) Rules 2015, not the ASCR; the instructing solicitor remains responsible for instructions, preparation, disclosure, service and advice. Never cite a Barristers rule against a solicitor or vice versa.
- **Breach, negligence and misconduct are not the same thing.** A missed deadline is a breach of the Rules; it is negligence only if it caused loss; it is misconduct only if it reaches section 296 or 297. The report labels which.

## Workflow

Work the steps in order. Each exists because skipping it has produced wrong reviews before: analysing against a superseded rule, citing a client duty against the other side's lawyer, or treating silence in a transcript as proof of breach.

### Step 1: Verify the standards are current (every time)

Follow Appendix G. Run the five quick checks (Uniform Law and General Rules; ASCR; Family Law Act; FCFCOA Act and Rules; any FCFCOA practice direction on AI or disclosure). Tell the user in one line if everything is still current. If something has changed, say what, and apply the version in force **at the date of the conduct**: conduct is judged by the rule that bound the lawyer at the time. Note the difference in the report's "Standards applied" section.

### Step 2: Obtain and inventory the source material

The review needs a primary source: a transcript, a judgment, the solicitor's filed documents, correspondence, a costs agreement and disclosure, bills, a trust statement or file notes. If none has been supplied, ask for it. Do not reconstruct conduct from the user's description alone; findings must cite a document.

- A PDF transcript is usually long. Read **all** of it before forming findings (use `Read` with `pages`, 20 at a time). Note the page and line convention and cite in that form throughout.
- Build a short inventory first: hearing or document date, judicial officer, appearances (who appeared for whom, counsel or solicitor), the stage (pre-action, interim, final, mention, costs), and the documents referred to on the record. This becomes the "Matter context" section and prevents attributing a submission to the wrong appearance.
- For a retainer review, collect the costs agreement, the costs disclosure, every bill, any estimate updates, and the engagement and termination letters. Dates matter: disclosure must precede the work.
- Check any matter notes the user has already provided before asking for context again.

### Step 3: Confirm the solicitor's identity, role and status

Identify from the appearances page, letterhead and headers who the solicitor is, which party they acted for, and whether counsel was briefed. Then confirm with the user using `AskUserQuestion` unless the user has already named them in this conversation or the project file does. Three things must be settled before analysis:

1. **Whose lawyer.** The user's current solicitor, the user's former solicitor, or the other party's solicitor. This fixes which domains apply (Domains 1 to 5 are client-side only).
2. **Solicitor or counsel on each occasion.** In-court conduct is attributed to whoever appeared. Counsel's conduct is reviewed against the Barristers Rules only where it bears on the solicitor (for example instructions given, or a failure to correct counsel's misstatement the solicitor knew to be wrong).
3. **Legal Aid or private.** A Legal Aid NSW grant brings the Panel Service Agreement and Quality Standards into play as a separate complaint pathway, and changes the costs-disclosure analysis.

### Step 4: Cross-reference against the framework

Go through Appendix A domain by domain. For each obligation record one of four classifications:

- **Compliant**: the source shows the duty being performed.
- **Breach**: the source shows the duty not performed or contravened, and the obligation is mandatory ("must").
- **Concern**: the conduct falls short of good practice, or breach is probable but the source is ambiguous, or the obligation is a guideline rather than a rule.
- **Not observable**: the duty is performed off the record (advice given, disclosure made, file management) and nothing in the source speaks to it either way. Say so; do not infer compliance or breach from silence.

Distinguish in-court conduct, which the transcript proves directly, from out-of-court duties, which it proves only when someone refers to them on the record (the judge asks "has your client been served?"; the solicitor says "we filed the financial statement on Friday"). Those references are the evidence; quote them.

Each finding needs: the exact quote (speaker, page and line, or document and paragraph), the provision, the classification, and one or two sentences on why it matters. Where a Rules obligation and an ASCR rule both apply, cite both: the Rules give the court its power, the ASCR gives the disciplinary body its standard.

Pay particular attention to the conduct most often found and most often missed:

- Statements to the court about what a document, report or affidavit says that the document does not support, and no correction when it becomes apparent (ASCR rules 19.1 to 19.3).
- Binding authority or legislation against the solicitor's position, which the other side has not raised, left undisclosed (ASCR rules 19.6 to 19.8), including the 2023 and 2024 Family Law Amendment Acts.
- Allegations of family violence, abuse or serious misconduct put in an affidavit or in cross-examination without a proper evidential basis (ASCR rules 21.1 to 21.4, 32).
- Financial disclosure incomplete, late or absent despite the continuing duty (Rules rule 6.01; FLA section 71B; Rules rule 6.06 undertaking), and material first produced at the hearing.
- Documents obtained by disclosure or subpoena used for a purpose outside the proceedings (the Harman undertaking; Hearne v Street), or the publication of proceedings identifying a party or child (FLA section 114Q, formerly section 121).
- A party's solicitor who never gave the mandatory information about non-court family services and the parenting-plan option (FLA sections 12E, 60D, 63DA), or never filed a genuine steps certificate or completed pre-action procedures (Rules rule 4.01 and Schedule 1).
- Costs disclosure not given, or not updated when the estimate changed substantially (Uniform Law sections 174 and 178), bills rendered without the required notification (section 192), or trust money applied to costs without authority (section 144, General Rules).
- A conflict: acting for both parties, acting against a former client on a related matter, or a personal interest in the outcome (ASCR rules 10 to 12), and the solicitor as a material witness (rule 27).
- Communication with the other party directly while they were represented (ASCR rule 33), or offensive or threatening correspondence to the other side (rules 32, 34).
- Failing to raise section 102NA when a self-represented party would otherwise cross-examine, or failing to inform the court of a family violence order, child protection notification or care arrangement (FLA sections 60CF to 60CI).
- Late service, late filing, duplicative evidence or adjournments that offend the overarching purpose (FCFCOA Act section 67; Rules rule 1.04), which can found a costs order against the lawyer personally (FCFCOA Act section 69; Rules Part 12).
- Generative AI used to prepare an affidavit, submission or list of authorities without verification, producing fictitious citations or unverified facts.
- Binding Financial Agreement advice without the independent-advice certificate requirements in FLA section 90G or 90UJ.

Conduct by *other* lawyers belongs in the report only where it bears on the solicitor under review (for example the other side's late service explains why the solicitor lacked a document, which counts for the solicitor, not against). Say in one sentence that it is not the subject of the review.

### Step 5: Classify the seriousness

For every Breach, say which of three consequences it supports, using Appendix B:

1. **Court consequence**: a costs order against the lawyer, an order for compliance, a rehearing ground. Needs a Rules or Act breach that affected the proceedings.
2. **Disciplinary consequence**: unsatisfactory professional conduct (section 296: conduct falling short of the standard of competence and diligence a member of the public is entitled to expect) or professional misconduct (section 297: substantial or consistent failure, or conduct justifying a finding the person is not a fit and proper person). Needs an ASCR or Uniform Law breach. Do not label a single lapse "professional misconduct"; the default for a single lapse is section 296.
3. **Civil consequence**: negligence or breach of retainer, where the breach caused identifiable loss. Note the advocate's immunity point: work intimately connected with the conduct of the case in court is immune (D'Orta-Ekenaike), but advice leading to a settlement is not (Attwells v Jackson Lalic Lawyers).

Keep the three distinct in the report: a Rules breach supports (1) and may support (2); only an ASCR or Uniform Law breach supports (2); (3) needs loss.

### Step 6: Check interpretation points against authority

When a finding depends on how a provision is read (what counts as misleading, the scope of the Harman undertaking, what full and frank disclosure requires, whether costs can be ordered against a lawyer, the limits of advocate's immunity), consult Appendix F and run one targeted web search for anything newer (search FCFCOA and NSWCATOD decisions for the provision number). Cite the authority with its medium-neutral citation. Do not pad with authority the finding does not need.

### Step 7: Write the report

Use Appendix H. The structure is fixed because the user reuses these reports as the spine of complaints and submissions:

1. **Matter context**: proceeding, event or documents reviewed, solicitor (confirmed) and whose lawyer, counsel if briefed, Legal Aid or private, standards applied with versions and currency confirmation.
2. **Summary verdict**: per domain, counts of compliant, breach, concern and not observable, and a two-sentence overall assessment.
3. **Findings table**: one row per obligation examined, with the consequence column (court, disciplinary, civil, none).
4. **Critical findings**: the three to five strongest items, each with the full quote, the provision, the seriousness classification and why it matters.
5. **Gaps and next steps**: what the source cannot show and what would resolve it (the solicitor's file under Uniform Law section 371 or a complaint, the court file, the trust ledger, further transcript days, a costs assessment under Part 4.3).
6. **Sources**: every document and authority relied on.

Tone: factual, measured, no adjectives doing the work a quote should do, no speculation about motive. Where the solicitor complied, say so plainly in the summary; the report is more persuasive when it is visibly balanced.

Deliverable format follows the user's stated preferences. Unless the user asks for a file, create the report as a Docs artifact (the user keeps and reuses these). When the review is to become a complaint or submission, hand the findings to the `complaint-letter-format` skill: each Critical finding becomes a Ground with its three lead-ins (what the document says, what the solicitor had, breach), the quotes go to Annexure A, and the comparison tables to Annexure B. Record a one-line summary of the review (date, event, headline findings) in the project's lodgement record if one exists and the review is for a live complaint.

## Common failure modes to avoid

- **Citing a client duty against the other side's lawyer.** The opponent's solicitor owes the user no duty of competence, advice or costs disclosure. Only rules 19 to 35 and the court's rules apply to them.
- **Treating silence as breach.** A transcript rarely shows whether advice was given or disclosure made. "Not observable" plus a next step is the right finding.
- **Confusing counsel with the solicitor.** Attribute in-court conduct to whoever was on their feet; the solicitor's accountability for counsel is a separate and narrower point.
- **Applying today's rule to yesterday's conduct.** Use the compilation in force at the date of the conduct; the ASCR changed on 1 July 2026 and the Family Law Act on 6 May 2024 and 10 June 2025.
- **Inflating seriousness.** A single lapse is section 296, not section 297. Say which and why.
- **Over-counting.** One act can breach several provisions; report it once with all the provisions, not three times.
- **Quoting guidelines as binding.** The Law Society and Family Law Section best-practice guidelines and the Legal Aid Quality Standards are practice standards, not rules. Frame each accordingly.

### Observability guide

| Usually visible on transcript or filed documents | Usually needs other evidence |
|---|---|
| Submissions, statements of fact, corrections | Advice given and when; instructions taken |
| Allegations put and their stated basis | Costs agreement, disclosure, bills, estimate updates |
| Service and filing dates (record and seals) | Trust ledger and statements |
| Whether a FV order, s 102NA or risk notice was raised | Conflict checks; former-client relationships |
| Disclosure complaints raised by the other side | What the solicitor held and when |
| Attendance and preparedness | File notes, supervision, AI use |
| Cross-examination conduct | Correspondence with the other side |

Silence on an "other evidence" item is **Not observable**, with a next step naming the document that would resolve it (the solicitor's file under a Uniform Law section 371 notice through the OLSC; a costs assessment under Part 4.3; the court file; the trust ledger through the Law Society's trust account investigators).

## Appendix B: Uniform Law discipline, costs and complaint pathways (NSW)

The Legal Profession Uniform Law is Schedule 1 to the Legal Profession Uniform Law Application Act 2014 (Vic), applied in NSW by the Legal Profession Uniform Law Application Act 2014 (NSW), commencing 1 July 2015. Cite as "section 296 of the Legal Profession Uniform Law (NSW)". Section numbers are the same in NSW, Victoria and WA.

### The two conduct categories

| Category | Section | Definition (paraphrased: quote the section in the report) | Typical findings |
|---|---|---|---|
| Unsatisfactory professional conduct | s 296 | Conduct in connection with the practice of law that falls short of the standard of competence and diligence a member of the public is entitled to expect of a reasonably competent lawyer | A single costs-disclosure failure; a missed deadline; a careless misstatement corrected late; discourtesy |
| Professional misconduct | s 297 | (a) UPC involving a **substantial or consistent** failure to reach or maintain a reasonable standard of competence and diligence; or (b) conduct, whether in connection with practice or not, that would justify a finding that the lawyer is **not a fit and proper person** | Knowingly misleading the court; trust defalcation; acting in a conflict for gain; a pattern of disclosure failures; dishonesty to the regulator |

Section 298 lists conduct **capable of** constituting either category: contravention of the Uniform Law or Uniform Rules; charging more than a fair and reasonable amount; conviction for a serious, tax or dishonesty offence; insolvency; failing to comply with a compensation order; failing to comply with a requirement to provide information or documents (s 298(f)).

**Classification rule.** A single lapse is s 296 unless it involves dishonesty, breach of trust obligations or knowing deception of the court, in which case s 297(b) is open. A pattern (three or more instances of the same failure, or failures across several domains) is s 297(a). State the limb and why in one sentence. Never write "professional misconduct" without naming the limb.

### Complaint pathways (NSW), kept distinct in the report

1. **OLSC**, Uniform Law Pt 5.2 and Ch 5. Consumer matter (costs disputes under $100,000, communication, delay: s 269), disciplinary matter (s 270), or both. Time limit 3 years from the conduct (s 272), extendable. Powers: investigation (Pt 5.4); requirement to provide information and documents (s 371; non-compliance is itself conduct under s 298(f)); determination (s 299: caution, reprimand, apology, redo work, training, fine up to $25,000); proceedings in NSWCATOD (s 300). The OLSC may refer a solicitor matter to the Law Society of NSW.
2. **Costs assessment**, Uniform Law Pt 4.3 Div 7 and the Application Act Pt 7: application within 12 months of the bill. Needs no misconduct; an unreasonable-costs finding may be referred to the OLSC.
3. **Legal Aid NSW**, Private Lawyer Quality Standards Unit, for a panel lawyer: breach of the Panel Service Agreement and Quality Standards. Audit, training, panel removal.
4. **The court**: costs against the lawyer personally (Act s 69; FLA s 117; R Pt 12), compliance orders, strike-out, appeal ground.
5. **Civil claim** for negligence or breach of retainer, subject to advocate's immunity and the Limitation Act 1969 (NSW) six-year period. Requires loss.

A Rules breach supports (4) and may support (1); only a Uniform Law or ASCR breach supports (1); (2) needs only unreasonableness; (5) needs loss.

### What a complaint to the OLSC needs

In writing, identifying the lawyer, the conduct, the dates and the documents; the complaint-letter-format skill governs layout. For each ground: document and pinpoint; what the lawyer had at the time; the provision; the category (s 296 or s 297 and limb); outcome sought (s 371 investigation, s 299 determination, s 300 referral, or a costs order). Where a fact is in the lawyer's file, say so and ask the Commissioner to obtain it under s 371.

### The Harman (implied) undertaking

A document obtained under compulsion (disclosure, subpoena, order, filed affidavit) may be used only for that litigation unless leave is granted or the document is read in open court. The obligation binds the party **and** the lawyer; breach is contempt and, for a lawyer, conduct capable of being professional misconduct. Authority: Hearne v Street [2008] HCA 36. It runs alongside FLA s 114Q (formerly s 121), which concerns publication to the public; Harman concerns any collateral use at all.
