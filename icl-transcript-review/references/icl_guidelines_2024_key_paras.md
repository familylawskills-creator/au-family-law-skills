# Guidelines for Independent Children's Lawyers (updated 2024) — key paragraphs

Published by National Legal Aid; endorsed by the Chief Justice of the FCFCOA and the Chief Judge of the Family Court of WA; current edition "updated 2024" (FCFCOA publication date 23 October 2024; FCWA page dated 1 November 2024). Source: fcfcoa.gov.au/fl/pubs/icl-guidelines (PDF and DOCX available). Confirmed current on 27 October 2026 — no later edition published.

**Status.** The Guidelines are not delegated legislation. They are the practice standard that (a) the court endorses, (b) every Legal Aid commission requires panel ICLs to comply with as a condition of panel membership (e.g. Legal Aid NSW Quality Standards — ICL Panel practice standards), and (c) disciplinary bodies apply when assessing whether an ICL met the standard of a competent practitioner in the role. Cite them as the standard; cite s 68LA and the Rules as the binding duty.

This file is a paragraph finder, not a substitute for the text. Paragraph numbers below follow the 2024 edition's structure; where the ICL's conduct turns on exact wording, download the PDF and quote it.

---

## 1–2 Purpose and the ICL's role in brief
- **1.1** Purpose: guidance to the ICL on fulfilling the role of representing and promoting the child's best interests.
- **2** Summary of the role: form an independent view; act in best interests; put the child's views fully before the court; meet the child and give them the opportunity to express views (unless under 5 / does not want to / exceptional circumstances); analyse reports and draw significant matters to the court's attention; minimise trauma; facilitate resolution where in best interests.

## 3 Appointment and the ICL's position in the proceedings
- **3.1** Circumstances for appointment (the *Re K* (1994) 17 Fam LR 537 categories remain the touchstone): allegations of abuse or neglect; high conflict; the child's views; family violence; mental health; proposed removal from the jurisdiction; sibling separation; neither parent suitable; a child of mature years expressing strong views; an SRL on each side; the ICL's own assessment of complexity.
- **3.1(h)** / settlement: where a proposed settlement significantly questions the child's best interests, the ICL should bring that to the court's attention.
- **3.2–3.4** Appointment mechanics; Legal Aid commission allocation; ICL as a party for procedural purposes.

## 4 Nature of the role — independence
- **4.1–4.2** Independent of the court, the parties and the funding body; forms their own view on the evidence.
- **4.3** Not the child's legal representative; not bound by instructions; must make that clear to the child and the parties.
- **4.4** Does not take instructions from, or act for, either party; avoids any appearance of alignment.

## 5 Skill in representing vulnerable children
- **5.1–5.4** Developmental stage, family structure, disability, cultural background (Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander children — Part VII s 60CC(3) considerations; CALD families; interpreters), LGBTQI+ children. Trauma-informed practice.

## 6 Relationships with the court, the parties and others
- **6.1–6.2** Relationship with the court: candour; the ICL assists the court; must not act as a second judge or a court agent.
- **6.3** Impartial dealings with parties; even-handed communications; no private communications that advantage one side.
- **6.4** Keeping parties informed of steps (expert instructions, subpoenas, proposals).
- **6.5** Self-represented litigants: explain the role; do not give legal advice; do not exploit.
- **6.6** Relationship with the Legal Aid commission; conflicts; reporting.

## 7 Conduct of the matter
- **7.1** Initial steps: obtain the court file; read all material; identify issues and risk.
- **7.2** Case plan; identify evidence needed; timetable; attend all events.
- **7.3** Explaining the role to the child; minimising trauma; limits of confidentiality.
- **7.4** Meeting with the child and providing the opportunity to express views
 - **7.4.1** Must meet and must give the opportunity (not necessarily at the same time); timing, frequency and method are for the ICL's professional judgment but must occur before final orders; the ICL should consider the most appropriate setting and whether a family consultant or child expert should be involved.
 - **7.4.2** Exceptional circumstances include (not limited to) exposing the child to a risk of physical or psychological harm that cannot be safely managed, or a significant adverse effect on the child's wellbeing.
 - **7.4.3** If the ICL considers exceptional circumstances exist, the court must decide before final orders whether they exist; if not, the court must order the ICL to meet/provide the opportunity.
 - **7.4.4** Where a parent obstructs the meeting, seek orders facilitating it (see *Tandy & Padula*).
- **7.5** Risk, family violence and child abuse: identify; obtain evidence (subpoenas to police, child protection, health, schools); ensure the court has what it needs for s 60CC(2)(a); s 102NA protections.
- **7.6** Expert evidence: family reports, Child Impact Reports, single experts; the ICL usually instructs; brief even-handedly; after the report, analyse it (s 68LA(5)(c)) and draw significant matters to the court's attention — including matters adverse to the ICL's own submission.
- **7.7** Confidentiality of the child's communications (s 68LA(6)–(8)); tell the child the limits in advance.
- **7.8** Communication with the child during proceedings; keeping the child informed.
- **7.9** Submissions: put a position where the evidence supports one (s 68LA(3)); **7.9.2** cross-examine where necessary to test evidence bearing on best interests.
- **7.10** The child's views
 - **7.10.1** Ensure views are fully put before the court (s 68LA(5)(b)) — fully means accurately and completely, not selectively.
 - **7.10.2** Views can be put through the ICL, a report, or other means (s 60CD); choose the mechanism that best preserves accuracy and minimises harm.
 - **7.10.3** The ICL's submission may differ from the child's views.
 - **7.10.4** Where it differs, the ICL should inform the court of the views and the reasons for departing from them, and explain the departure to the child in an age-appropriate way.
 - **7.10.5** Whether the child should give evidence or meet the judge: the ICL advises the court (r 8.10).
- **7.11** Interim hearings: the ICL should be in a position to put relevant evidence (including subpoenaed material) before the court at interim hearings; interim safety arrangements.
- **7.12** Negotiation and settlement: facilitate where in best interests (s 68LA(5)(e)); **7.12.2** do not consent to orders that significantly question the child's best interests without bringing that to the court's attention.
- **7.13** Explaining the outcome to the child.
- **7.14** Discharge: apply where the ICL cannot perform the role (conflict; child refuses; parental obstruction; relationship breakdown); do not simply continue ineffectively.
- **7.15** Costs: Legal Aid funding; applications for contribution; the Rules' costs statement requirement.

## 8 Other proceedings
- Hague Convention proceedings (s 68L now applies without the "exceptional circumstances" gate); child support; state child welfare interaction; Magellan/Evatt lists.
