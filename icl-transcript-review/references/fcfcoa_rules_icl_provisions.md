The FCFCOA (Division 2) (Family Law) Rules 2021 adopt these Rules for Division 2 with minor modifications (costs scale). For the Family Court of WA, use the Family Court Rules 2021 (WA).

This file summarises the operative content of each rule with its mapping to the framework. Quote the rule itself from the compilation (legislation.gov.au/F2021L01197/latest/text) when the exact wording matters.

## r 1.04 — Overarching purpose
The overarching purpose of the Rules is to facilitate the just resolution of disputes according to law, as quickly, inexpensively and efficiently as possible, and in family law proceedings with the best interests of the child as the paramount consideration; parties and their lawyers must conduct proceedings consistently with it (FCFCOA Act 2021 ss 67–68, 190–191). → Framework 6.3.

## r 2.25–2.27 — Service
- r 2.27(3)(b): a party must serve the ICL with every document filed. A party's failure to do so is the party's non-compliance, not the ICL's. → Framework 7.6.

## Part 3.4 — r 3.11 Independent children's lawyer
- (1) A party may apply for appointment or removal of an ICL by Application in a Proceeding (the court may also act on its own motion under s 68L).
- (2) On appointment the court may (a) request the Legal Aid commission to arrange the representation; (b) order that the ICL's costs be met by one or more parties.
- (3) A person appointed as ICL: (a) must file a Notice of Address for Service; (b) must comply with the obligations of a party under the Rules; (c) is subject to the duty of disclosure (Part 6.1) as a party.
- (4)–(5) Costs statement and cessation: an ICL must, not less than 1 day before the first day of trial, file and serve a written statement of the actual costs incurred up to and including the trial (where a costs contribution may be sought); the appointment ceases when the order provides, on final orders (unless the order continues it), or on discharge.
Note: s 68L provides for independent representation; s 68LA sets out the role.
→ Framework 7.1–7.5, 4.5.

## Part 6.1 — Duty of disclosure
- r 6.01: each party has a continuing duty of full and frank disclosure of all information relevant to the proceeding, timely and in the required form. Applies to the ICL by r 3.11(3)(c). → Framework 4.5.
- rr 6.02–6.05: disclosure undertakings and mechanics.

## Part 6.3 — Subpoenas (rr 6.26–6.42)
- r 6.27(1): a party may request a subpoena; (2) a party or ICL must not request the issue of a subpoena for an interim hearing without leave — **except** that (3)–(4) the restriction does not apply to the ICL requesting a subpoena for the production of documents. Practically: the ICL has unrestricted capacity to subpoena documents at interlocutory stages and is expected to use it where risk evidence is held by third parties. Limit on number of subpoenas per party (five without leave) applies to parties; check the current compilation for the ICL carve-out wording.
- rr 6.29–6.36: service, objections, inspection, copying; the ICL is entitled to inspect produced material.
→ Framework 4.3, 5.1, 6.2 (interim hearings).

## Part 7 — Expert evidence (single experts)
- r 7.03–7.08: single expert appointment; the ICL commonly instructs or is a joint instructing party.
- r 7.17: parties' communications with the single expert are restricted; r 7.18(2): nothing prevents the ICL communicating with a single expert (the ICL's communications should nonetheless be disclosed to parties — Guidelines 6.4). → Framework 4.2, 4.6.

## r 8.10 — Evidence of a child
A child must not give evidence, be called, or swear an affidavit without leave; the ICL advises the court on the appropriate means of putting the child's views (s 60CD). → Framework 3.9.

## r 10.18 — Undertakings
Undertakings to the court must be in writing, signed, filed (or recorded on the record and reduced to writing as directed). An ICL-proposed undertaking that is never filed has no effect. → Framework 6.5.

## Part 12 — Costs
- s 117 FLA governs; r 12.xx (current numbering) addresses costs applications. An application for parties to contribute to the ICL's costs requires the r 3.11 costs statement. See *Winston & Winston* [2026] FedCFamC1A 88 (costs ordered against a mother whose conduct prevented the ICL performing the statutory function; application against the father dismissed). → Framework 7.4.
