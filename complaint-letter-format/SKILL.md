# Complaint and report format

This is a standard format for complaints and submissions. Every report, letter, complaint or submission written for the user follows it, whatever the recipient. Other skills that produce reports for the user (single-expert-report-review, icl-transcript-review, party-solicitor-conduct-review) hand their findings to this format rather than their own.

The point of the format is that a complaints solicitor can check every sentence against a document and a provision without reading anything twice. Precision and short paragraphs matter more than polish.

## 1. Layout, top to bottom

1. **Sender block**, one item per line: full name, street, suburb state postcode, email, mobile.
2. **Date** as `dd/mm/yyyy` on its own line.
3. **Addressee block**, one item per line: unit or department, organisation, street, suburb state postcode, `By email: <address>`, and `Copy: <body>, <address>` if anyone is copied.
4. **Subject**, bold, two lines: line 1 is the reference and who it is about (`Complaint [reference] ([regulator file ref]): [Name], solicitor, [firm]`); line 2 is what the document is (`Supplementary submission, corrections and further documents`).
5. `To whom it may concern,`
6. **Opening paragraph**: who the user is in the matter, the matter name and file number, the court and division, the children by first name or an agreed pseudonym with dates of birth where age matters, and the person complained of with their role. Where the person was not the user's lawyer, say so in one sentence (`[Name] did not act for me or for the other party; an ICL acts for neither party.`).
7. **Scope sentence**, underlined: `This letter does three things;` followed by a numbered list of exactly three items, each a short clause ending in a full stop.
8. `Annexure A - <title>` and `Annexure B - <title>` on separate lines.
9. Any procedural note (why sending now, that the user has not raised it with the person, why) as one or two short paragraphs.
10. `## Part 1.`, `## Part 2.`, `## Part 3.` headings. Part 1 is context or corrections, Part 2 is the grounds, Part 3 is documents enclosed and outcome sought.
11. Closing: availability line (`I am available on <mobile> between 2 pm and 5 pm or email: <address> at any time.`), then `I confirm that the information in this letter is true to the best of my knowledge.`, then `Yours faithfully`, then full name.
12. `## Annexure A. Verbatim extracts`, then `## Annexure B. <tables>`.

## 2. Grounds (Part 2)

Introduce Part 2 with one sentence stating the three-step order and that quotations are in Annexure A.

Each ground is a `###` heading: `Ground A. <the failure, stated as a fact>` (for example `Ground D. The case outline and tender bundle were served six minutes before the listing`). Letters, not numbers.

Inside each ground, three bold lead-ins in this order, each starting its own paragraph:

- `**What the <document> says.**` (or `**What the transcript says.**`, `**What the law requires.**`): quote the document, naming the part and paragraph (`Part D paragraph 11`, `paragraph 41 of the report`, `page 1, in the court's own form instructions`).
- `**What <the person> had.**` (or `**What I know.**`): what was available to them at the time, as bullets, each bullet a dated document or fact, with nested sub-bullets for the detail inside it.
- `**Breach.**` (or `**Breach alleged, and request.**` where the fact is not yet proven): what follows, then the provisions.

Rules inside a ground:

- One point per paragraph. Short paragraphs, often one sentence. Sub-points as nested bullets rather than long sentences.
- A list is introduced with a semicolon on the lead line (`That is a failure to form an independent view based on;`), each item ends with a comma, `and` on the second-last item, full stop on the last.
- Key sentences are underlined, not bolded. Bold is for the three lead-ins and the subject only.
- Verbatim quotations are in italics inside double quotes, followed in brackets by the pinpoint and the annexure extract: `(transcript page 5, lines 14 to 19; Annexure A, extract 1)`.
- Every provision names its Act or rules in full the first time in each ground, with the section in words: `section 68LA(5)(c) of the Family Law Act 1975 (Cth)`, `rule 19.1 of the Legal Profession Uniform Law Australian Solicitors' Conduct Rules 2015`, `paragraph 7.4 of the Guidelines for Independent Children's Lawyers (2024)`. Never a bare `s 68LA`.
- For a Legal Aid NSW letter, end each Breach step with `Quality Standards not met;` and a list of standard numbers with a short bracketed description.
- Where a fact can only be proven from a file the user cannot see, say so (`I cannot see [Name]'s file.`) and ask the body to obtain it, naming the power (`under clause 9.2 of the Panel Service Agreement`, `under section 371 of the Legal Profession Uniform Law (NSW)`).
- Where the user did not hold a document and recorded it by hand (a subpoena inspection room), say so, enclose the user's typed and signed note, and ask the body to obtain the original from whoever holds it. Never describe notes as extracts or copies.
- Conduct of other people (counsel, the successor lawyer) is mentioned only where it bears on the person complained of, with a sentence making clear it is not the subject of this complaint.

## 3. Part 3: documents enclosed and outcome sought

A table with four columns: `#`, `Document` (full description with date, seal time where relevant, and page count in brackets), `Pages that support the complaint` (the pinpoints), `Ground`. Rows do not split across pages; the header row repeats.

Keep enclosure numbering identical across every body that receives the same material, so the Law Society, Legal Aid and anyone else work from one bundle. New items for a later recipient are appended (13, 14, 15), not renumbered. A document that exists in an original and an annotated form is enclosed as both: the original as filed is `1`, the annotated copy is `1A`, with a sentence saying nothing else is altered.

Then a bold sentence `I ask that <body>:` and a numbered list of the outcomes sought, each a verb clause ending in a semicolon, `and` on the second-last, full stop on the last. Requests for production list the documents as nested bullets with the ground in brackets. A sentence consenting to the bodies exchanging material with each other where more than one is involved.

## 4. Annexures

- **Annexure A. Verbatim extracts.** One line naming the source and who the speakers are. Then `**Extract n. Transcript page x, lines y to z (Ground A)**` and the passage as a block quote, verbatim, with `...` for omissions. Extracts from documents other than the transcript follow the same pattern (`Extract 9. Outline of Case Document, Part D paragraph 11 (Ground A)`).
- **Annexure B.** Tables that compare what a document says with what the source shows, column by column, followed by one or two sentences stating the count (`Of the eight entries, three carry a date that does not appear on the source document`). Where the table rests on the user's own inspection notes, a closing paragraph says so and lists anything further the inspection showed.

## 5. Enclosure bundle

One PDF, each enclosure preceded by an A4 cover sheet carrying the file reference, the user's name and the letter date at the top, `Enclosure n` large in the centre, and the document's full title beneath. Enclosures are also supplied as individually named files in a zip (`Enclosure_03_Short_title.pdf`). Emails are printed to PDF with the From, To, Sent and Subject header block and the full chain. A note typed from the user's handwritten record ends with `I confirm that this is a true typed copy of the notes I made from <source>.`, the user's name and the date, for signature.

## 6. Style

- No em dashes anywhere; use a comma, colon or new sentence. Titles use a colon, not a dash.
- Children by first name or pseudonym, never initials. Dates as `3 March 2021`. Times as `9.30 am`. Ranges as `lines 14 to 19`, `pages 5 to 9`, `paragraphs 70 to 72`.
- `paragraph 88.2`, `Section A, page 3`, never `at 88.2` or `p 3`.
- No adjectives doing the work a quotation should do; no speculation about motive; describe the conduct and its effect.
- It must not read like AI. Plain sentences, the user's register, no summarising flourish at the end of a ground.
- Square brackets mark a fact only the user can confirm; the sending version has none left, and the drafting header (`Draft for [name] to review`) and byline are removed.

## 7. Producing the file

Deliver as DOCX and PDF together. Build the DOCX from the previous letter as the style template (pandoc `--reference-doc` on the user's last sent letter, or edit a copy of it with python-docx) so fonts, headings and table borders match. Pandoc loses pipe tables when the reference document is a Word file; copy the Part 3 and Annexure B tables from the previous DOCX with python-docx and edit the cells, rather than regenerating them. Set `cantSplit` on every row and `tblHeader` on the first. Convert to PDF with LibreOffice headless and look at the first page, the Part 3 table and the last annexure page before sending. Check the file reference number against the regulator's own letter, not the web-form ID.

With the files, draft the cover email: subject `<Reference> - <person> - <what it is>`, body naming the complaint and its date, what is attached with page counts, any timing note, and the user's signature block. Record the lodgement (date, time, addressees, attachments, outcome sought, open items) in the project's lodgement record.
