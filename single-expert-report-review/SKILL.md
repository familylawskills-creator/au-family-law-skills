# Single Expert and Court Report Review — FCFCOA parenting proceedings

Use this when asked to review, audit, check compliance of, score, or challenge a single expert witness (SEW) report written by a psychiatrist in Australian family law (children's) proceedings, or to draft a complaint about the expert to the NSW Health Care Complaints Commission (HCCC) / Medical Council. Also use it for a report by a court report writer who is a psychologist or social worker: a Child Impact Report, Child Impact Addendum Report, Specific Issues Report, Family Report or Update Family Report by a Court Child Expert or Panel Family Consultant, or a private family report by a psychologist or social worker. For those, start at "Court report writers" below, because different rules bind the author. Produces: (1) a chat checklist, (2) a formal review report as a Claude Docs document, (3) draft r 7.26 clarification questions, (4) a challenge plan and, when asked, (5) a regulator complaint written to the conventions in "Writing conventions (mandatory)" below.

## Benchmarks

1. **Federal Circuit and Family Court of Australia (Family Law) Rules 2021, Part 7.1** (Divisions 7.1.1–7.1.7). Use the compilation in force on the review date. As at 26 November 2026 that is Compilation No. 4 (compilation date 10 June 2025, F2025C00594); the Part 7.1 and r 8.11 pinpoints in this skill were checked against it on that date. Check for a later compilation every time.
2. **Family Law Act 1975 (Cth)** — Part VII, esp. s 60CC (best interests, as amended by the Family Law Amendment Act 2023 from 6 May 2024), s 60CG (family violence), s 62G (family report orders), **Subdivision DA ss 67ZBC–67ZBK** (information sharing orders — replaced s 69ZW from 6 May 2024), s 69ZT (Evidence Act provisions switched off in child-related proceedings), **Part XIVB ss 114N–114S** (communication of details of proceedings — replaced the former s 121), *Family Law Regulations 2024* s 54 (information sharing safeguards; this was reg 12CBB of the repealed *Family Law Regulations 1984* until 1 April 2025).
3. **RANZCP PPG3** — Australian Family Court proceedings (Feb 2019).
4. **RANZCP PPG11** — Developing reports and conducting independent medical examinations in medico-legal settings (Nov 2020).
5. **Health Practitioner Regulation National Law (NSW)** — for a regulator complaint: s 139B(1)(a) (knowledge, skill, judgment or care significantly below the standard reasonably expected), s 139B(1)(l) (other improper or unethical conduct relating to practice), s 139E (professional misconduct: more than one instance of unsatisfactory professional conduct that together is serious enough to justify suspension or cancellation). s 139C is NOT professional misconduct; do not cite it as such. **Health Care Complaints Act 1993 (NSW)** ss 7, 23. **Medical Board of Australia, Good medical practice (2020)** s. 10.8–10.9 (medico-legal reports). Verify wording against the current legislation text, never memory.

**Old references.** PPG3 cites the repealed *Family Law Rules 2004* Part 15.5. Always translate: 15.44→7.03, 15.45→7.04, 15.47→7.06, 15.52–15.58→7.13–7.19, 15.59→7.21, 15.62–15.66→7.23–7.27. In the Act: "s 121" → Part XIVB; "s 69ZW" → ss 67ZBD (particulars order) / 67ZBE (documents or information order). Never cite a 2004 rule number, s 121 or s 69ZW as current law in output; if the report uses them, note it as a terminology defect (Minor) and check whether the underlying material was obtained under the current provision. The *Family Law Regulations 1984* were replaced by the *Family Law Regulations 2024* on 1 April 2025: "reg 12CBB" → s 54 (information sharing safeguards); "reg 7" / "Regulation 7 family consultant" → s 40 / "Panel Family Consultant" (the Court's fact sheets say "regulation 40"). Verify both numbers against the live Regulations before citing.

## Before scoring — four gate checks

1. **Report type.** Confirm it is a Part 7.1 SEW report (jointly agreed under r 7.03 or court-ordered under r 7.04). A report by a family consultant (Court Child Expert or Panel Family Consultant) under s 62G, s 55A or s 11F is a different regime: Part 7.1 does not apply to it except r 7.14 (r 7.01(1)(d)). Say so and run the "Court report writers" section below instead of Domains 1 and 2. A treating practitioner's evidence (r 7.01(1)(a)) is also outside Part 7.1; limit that audit to clinical quality and s 60CC reasoning. If the author is a psychologist or social worker appointed as a single expert, Part 7.1 applies in full, with the profession's own standards substituted for RANZCP (see Track B below).
2. **Currency.** Verify, with web search, that PPG3 and PPG11 are still the current RANZCP versions and fetch the current Part 7.1 compilation from legislation.gov.au. Quote the r 7.25/7.26/7.27 time limits from the live text, not memory. Note any change in the output's "Deadlines" section. PPG11's own stated review date (November 2023) has passed, so check for a replacement every time. Also check whether regulations for family report writers have been made under s 11K of the Family Law Act (Part IIIAA). As at 26 November 2026 none were located (re-checked on legislation.gov.au and the Attorney-General's Department site), so s 11K is still only a power to make regulations: it permits a regulator, a complaints process, recognition of compliant writers and penalties of up to 30 penalty units (s 11K(2) and (4)), and none of that exists yet. Do not cite family report writer standards or a complaints scheme under Part IIIAA as being in force; say the framework is legislated and the regulations are not yet made.
3. **Admissibility framing.** In child-related proceedings s 69ZT generally disapplies the Evidence Act opinion and hearsay rules unless the court orders otherwise. Defects therefore usually go to **weight and r 7.23 sanctions**, not admissibility. Do not describe a report as "inadmissible"; use "serious weight / sanction exposure".
4. **Release restriction.** Before quoting the report or offering any output that leaves the reviewing party and their lawyer, read the appointing order, any order releasing the report and any cover notice for a limit on who may have the report or see its contents. State the result in the first lines of the review: the order number and what it restricts, or "no restriction found in the documents supplied", or "Not assessable — request the release order". If a restriction exists or cannot be ruled out, do not offer to draft a regulator complaint, r 7.26 questions for sending, or any extract for a third party without saying first that the Court's permission may be needed (see "Confidentiality" in Rules of engagement).

## Court report writers — Child Impact Reports, family reports, and psychologist or social worker experts

Use this section when the author is not a psychiatrist single expert. Work through Steps A to F, then return to the main Workflow for outputs. The Writing conventions below still apply to everything produced.

### Step A — Identify the track (this decides which rules bind)

| Track | Author | How appointed | What binds the author |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **A. Family consultant** | Court Child Expert (court employee in the Court Children's Service, a psychologist or social worker) or Panel Family Consultant (private practitioner appointed by the CEO under *Family Law Regulations 2024* s 40; formerly "Regulation 7") | Court direction under s 62G(2) or s 55A(2); attendance orders under s 11F or s 62G(5); application under r 8.11 | FLA Part III (ss 11A to 11G), s 62G, r 8.11, the order itself, Australian Standards of Practice (2015), CCS clinical governance, and the author's professional code. **Part 7.1 does not apply except r 7.14** (r 7.01(1)(d)). |
| **B. Private single expert (psychologist or social worker)** | Private practitioner | Jointly under r 7.03 or by order under r 7.04 | Part 7.1 in full. Run Domains 1 to 5 as written, replacing RANZCP PPG3/PPG11 with the Australian Standards of Practice (2015) and the profession code in Step B. |
| **C. Other private report** | Report commissioned for an ICL (s 68M(2)) or by one party | Varies | If tendered as expert opinion, Part 7.1 applies (permission under Division 7.1.3, disclosure under r 7.14). Check how it came before the court before scoring. |

All three are "designated family reports" if they follow a family assessment and give expert views on parenting arrangements (s 11J), so the author is a "family report writer" (s 11H). That label carries no enforceable standards until regulations are made under s 11K (gate check 2).

**Report types under Track A** (from the Court's fact sheet "Reports prepared by Court Children's Service"; re-read it on each review):
- **Child Impact Report (CIR):** preliminary, child-focused, for interim hearings and dispute resolution. Usually one interview per party (often by video) and a child session. The Court itself says it is not an assessment suited to a final hearing or to cross-examination.
- **Child Impact Addendum Report:** same author, within six months of the CIR's release, limited further enquiries, may make recommendations for final hearing. Not for disputed risk.
- **Specific Issues Report:** one or two issues named in the order. No opinion beyond them.
- **Family Report:** comprehensive s 60CC analysis with recommendations for final hearing. The Court says it cannot give a clinical assessment or diagnosis of mental health.
- **Update Family Report:** same author, limited to changes since the Family Report.

### Step B — Benchmarks for these tracks

1. **Family Law Act 1975 (Cth):** s 11A (functions), s 11B (who is a family consultant), s 11C (communications are admissible and not confidential, but only if the person was first told so: s 11C(2); abuse disclosures excepted: s 11C(3)), s 11D (same immunity as a judge; s 11D(2) reserves s 11K obligations), s 11E, s 11F and s 11G (attendance orders and consequences), s 62G(2) (direction), **s 62G(3A) (must ascertain the child's views on the matter and include them)**, s 62G(3B) (unless inappropriate for age, maturity or special circumstance), s 62G(4) (may add other welfare matters), s 62G(6) (must report non-attendance), s 62G(8) (may be received in evidence), s 60CE (child cannot be required to express views), s 60CC, s 67ZA (family consultant must notify a child welfare authority on reasonable suspicion of abuse), Part XIVB.
2. **Rules:** r 8.11(1) and note (a family consultant's report may be a child impact report, specific issues report or family report), r 8.11(2) (factors for ordering), **r 8.11(3) (the application and the order must identify the issues the report is to address)**, r 8.11(4), r 8.11(5) (release, receipt in evidence, oral examination, restriction of access), r 8.11(6) (the notice that must go with a copy the court gives out otherwise than by order), r 7.01(1)(d), r 7.14.
3. **Australian Standards of Practice for Family Assessments and Reporting (February 2015):** 41 standards. Not legally binding, pre-dates the 2021 court merger, but endorsed by the courts and the usual yardstick for all tracks. Take standard numbers from the PDF on the review date, never from memory.
4. **Psychologists (registered):** Psychology Board of Australia, *Code of conduct for psychologists*, in effect 1 December 2025. Most relevant: 8.8 (legal, insurance and other assessments), 8.9 (reports, certificates and giving evidence), 8.12 (conflicts of interest), 8.3 (integrity of assessment methods), 8.5 (client records), 4.2 (informed consent), 4.3 (children and young people), 4.4 (associated parties), 4.9 (multiple relationships), 3.3 (privacy and confidentiality), 2.2 and 3.1 (cultural safety). **Conduct before 1 December 2025 is assessed against the APS Code of Ethics (2007)**, so date every act before choosing the code. Check the clause wording live.
5. **Social workers (not registered with Ahpra):** AASW *Code of Ethics 2020*. It binds AASW members only (section 10.1). Clauses that matter for a court report, checked against the Code's text:
   - **5.5.6 (the key clause):** every prepared report, including one for legal purposes, must separate fact from opinion, must not deliberately omit a relevant fact, and must rest its conclusions on thorough assessment and appropriate evidence.
   - **5.4.3:** before an interview starts, tell the person if what they say will be used for assessment and given to third parties for legal reasons. Pair with FLA s 11C(2).
   - **5.7.13:** where the worker expects conflicting roles (the Code's example is testifying in court proceedings), clarify the role with the parties and minimise the conflict. **5.7.12:** clarify obligations where the people served are related to each other.
   - **4.5 Conflicts of interest** (unbiased judgment) and **4.4 Professional competence** (evidence-informed practice), **4.1 Cultural safety and sensitivity**, **5.5 Record management** generally.
   - **Numbering trap:** in the Code's PDF the contents page is one number behind the body for section 4 (contents: 4.3 competence, 4.4 conflicts; body: 4.4 competence, 4.5 conflicts). Cite the body number together with the heading, for example "section 4.5 (Conflicts of interest)", in APA form (Australian Association of Social Workers, 2020, s. 5.5.6).
   - Also check for the AASW ethics guideline on writing court reports.
   - In NSW also the *Code of conduct for non-registered health practitioners* (*Public Health Regulation 2022* (NSW) Sch 3); that code needs its own review and is outside this skill.
6. **Family violence and culture:** s 4AB, s 60CC(2)(a) and (3), s 60CG, and the Standards on family violence assessment and on Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander families.

### Step C — Gate checks for Track A

1. **Get the order.** Without the s 62G or s 11F order and the r 8.11(3) issues, mark scope checks "Not assessable — request the order".
2. **Report type against use.** Identify the type from the report's own title and the order. A CIR relied on for final orders, or a Specific Issues Report used beyond its issue, is a use problem to raise with the court. It is not of itself misconduct by the author.
3. **Authorship and timing.** Court Child Expert or Panel Family Consultant; for an Addendum or Update, same author and (Addendum) within six months.
4. **Immunity (s 11D).** Do not suggest a civil claim against a family consultant. Whether immunity affects a regulator's or professional body's power to act on a court-ordered report is a legal question: flag it for the lawyer, do not assert either way.
5. **Release restrictions.** See Step F before quoting anything.
6. **Live or finished proceedings.** The Court will not investigate a complaint about a family consultant while the matter is before it. Record which stage the matter is at.

### Step D — Checklist for Track A (replaces Domains 1 and 2; Domains 3 to 5 still run where noted)

Score as usual: Compliant / Partial / Non-compliant / Not assessable, each with a quote and the exact provision or standard.

**Domain F1 — Order, scope and form**
- Report answers each issue identified in the order (r 8.11(3)) and does not go beyond them without saying why (s 62G(4) allows other care, welfare or development matters only)
- Report type matches the order; a CIR stays preliminary (screening and early guidance) and does not present final parenting recommendations as settled conclusions
- Author's qualifications, role (family consultant) and the date and mode of every interview and observation stated
- Documents read are listed, with dates; subpoena material read only where the court directed it
- Stated limitations match the report type (single video interview, no home visit, no collateral contact)
- Proofed: names, dates, children's ages and who said what are internally consistent

**Domain F2 — Statutory duties**
- Each person interviewed was told beforehand that nothing said is confidential and that it is admissible (s 11C(2)); the report records this
- Child's views on each matter ascertained and included (s 62G(3A)), or the report states why that was inappropriate (s 62G(3B)); no pressure on the child to state a preference (s 60CE)
- Non-attendance or non-compliance reported to the court (s 62G(6))
- Where the report records a reasonable suspicion of abuse or risk, a notification is recorded (s 67ZA) and stated in the report
- No family counselling or dispute resolution role held by the same person in the same matter

**Domain F3 — Fair process (Australian Standards of Practice 2015)**
- Impartiality; any prior or current relationship with a party disclosed, or the assessment declined
- Each parent or carer interviewed, separately, with comparable time and method; not telephone-only or joint-only; any difference between the parties' treatment explained
- Every allegation the author relies on was put to the person it concerns for a response, or the report says why not
- Collateral sources identified, obtained with permission or under direction, and open to challenge
- Safety arrangements made where family violence is alleged; no joint attendance that exposes a party to risk
- Interpreter, literacy and disability needs met
- No advice, therapy or settlement brokering during the assessment; no opinion formed before the assessment; no contact with a party after the report except as permitted
- Contemporaneous notes kept; no covert observation or recording

**Domain F4 — Children**
- Each child assessed individually, in person where possible, in a private and safe setting, with methods suited to age and language
- The report says how the views were obtained, the child's maturity, and the weight given to the views
- Parent and child observations done for each caregiver, or the omission explained
- Sibling relationships and the risk to the child of reporting their views considered

**Domain F5 — Risk, family violence and expertise**
- Risk assessment rests on enough information, and is expressed as conditional on the court's findings about disputed facts; allegation, investigation outcome and finding are kept distinct (apply Domain 4A to any police or child protection material)
- Family violence assessed with a recognised framework where alleged; no minimising, no treating allegations as mere conflict without reasons
- **No diagnosis or clinical mental health opinion by an author not qualified and instructed to give one**; opinions confined to demonstrated competence
- Psychometric tools only with training, for a validated purpose, and reconciled with other data
- Cultural background, and for an Aboriginal or Torres Strait Islander child the matters in s 60CC(3), addressed

**Domain F6 — Reasoning and recommendations**
- Run Domain 3 (objectivity; facts that detract from the opinion) as a professional-standard check, citing the Standards and the profession code, not r 7.18. For a social worker the direct equivalent is AASW Code 5.5.6 (fact separated from opinion; no relevant fact deliberately omitted; conclusions based on thorough assessment and evidence)
- Run Domain 5 in full (s 60CC, s 60CG, each recommendation traced to data)
- Information, inference and opinion are distinguished; limitations stated; where data are insufficient the author declines to give an opinion on arrangements
- Recommendations to the court kept separate from suggestions to the parties; risks of each option identified; research cited fairly, including research against the author's view

**Severity for Track A**
- **Critical:** person not told of s 11C before interview; child's views missing with no s 62G(3B) reason; an allegation relied on that was never put to the party; undisclosed relationship with a party; opinion on a parent or household the author did not assess; clinical diagnosis outside the author's competence; final-arrangement recommendations in a CIR with no supporting assessment; a recorded suspicion of abuse with no notification; a relevant fact the author held and left out.
- **Notable:** unequal interview time or mode unexplained; collateral sources unnamed; family violence alleged and not assessed; limitations unstated; child's views reported with no maturity or weight analysis; report strays outside the r 8.11(3) issues; fact and opinion not separated.
- **Minor:** proofing errors, outdated terminology ("Regulation 7", s 121, s 69ZW, 1984 regulation numbers).

### Step E — Challenge routes for Track A (use instead of Step 5 items 1 to 6; label as advocacy)

1. **Within the proceedings first.** The Court's complaints policy says this is the proper forum. Affidavit evidence in answer, and submissions on weight: the court is not bound by the report and it is one source of evidence.
2. **Cross-examination.** Written notice to the family consultant at least 14 days before the hearing (Court's Family Reports FAQ and complaints policy; r 7.09 itself does not apply). Oral examination is by the court's permission (r 8.11(5)(c)). A CIR is prepared for an interim event, so cross-examination on it is unusual: raise concerns by affidavit and submissions, and ask for a fuller report.
3. **Written questions need leave.** r 7.25 and r 7.26 do not apply to a family consultant (r 7.01(1)(d)). Draft "proposed questions, subject to leave" or, more usefully, cross-examination topics.
4. **Ask for another report.** Family Report, Update Family Report or Specific Issues Report under r 8.11, identifying the issues (r 8.11(3)); or a single expert under Part 7.1 where a clinical question needs a psychologist or psychiatrist (cost is a r 8.11(2)(c) factor).
5. **Further directions** about preparation of the report (s 62G(7)), or an application within the proceedings to have a different family consultant assigned. A complaint cannot achieve either.
6. **Complaint to the Court Children's Service.** Email customer.service@fcfcoa.gov.au with name, postal address, phone, case name and file number. A senior CCS officer reviews it and replies in writing. It is not investigated, or put to the consultant, while the matter is before the Court. The Court aims to respond within 20 working days. A complaint cannot stay proceedings or change orders. Verify the policy text live.
7. **Professional body or regulator** (after proceedings end, or expect it to be deferred or declined while they are live):
   - **HCCC rule for every profession (psychiatrist, psychologist or social worker): conduct, not content.** The Commission's own guidance says it cannot help with a complaint about the content of a medico-legal or Family Court report, may decide to take no further action under *Health Care Complaints Act 1993* (NSW) s 27(1)(c), and sends people to the body that asked for the report. It says it can consider the professional conduct of the practitioner who carried out the assessment. So write each ground as something the author did or failed to do (did not warn before interview, did not put an allegation, left out a fact they held, gave an opinion outside competence, did not disclose a relationship). Never write a ground as "the finding is wrong" or "the recommendation is unfair". Re-read the HCCC page on the day.
   - *Psychologist in NSW:* HCCC and the Psychology Council of NSW. The National Law (NSW) s 139B(1)(a), s 139B(1)(l) and s 139E apply as for a psychiatrist; cite the Psychology Board code clauses (or APS Code for pre-December 2025 conduct) in place of the Medical Board code. Other States: Ahpra (Queensland: Office of the Health Ombudsman first).
   - *Social worker, HCCC:* not a registered health practitioner, so **do not cite s 139B or s 139E**. The route is the non-registered practitioners' code (Sch 3). Present it as a conduct complaint and let the Commission decide whether it will deal with it.
   - *Social worker, AASW:* only if the person is a member, so phone the Ethics Office (03 9320 1000) to check membership first. Complaints go on the online Ethics Complaints Management Process form, measured against the Code of Ethics 2020 under the AASW By-Laws on Ethics 2025 (the form asks you to confirm you have read By-Law 6.2.4). Know before lodging: (a) the complaint, your name and every attachment may be sent to the social worker; (b) the AASW says it cannot use documents that are confidential, private or owned by another body unless that body signs a release, and it names a court or registrar as an example, so a court report needs the Court's release first (Step F); (c) a complaint for someone else needs their signed third-party authorisation; (d) the form asks whether you have complained to another body; (e) further material goes to ethics.complaint@aasw.asn.au; (f) outcomes run from no action or conciliation to caution, practice conditions, suspension or termination of membership. The AASW cannot affect court orders.
   - *Private writer (Tracks B and C):* the Court's complaints policy does not cover them. Within the proceedings, then the professional body.

### Step F — Outputs and release restrictions for these tracks

- Same two outputs as Step 4. Title the Claude Doc "Court Report Compliance Review — [author] ([report type], [report date])" and use Domains F1 to F6 for Track A. Replace "Draft r 7.26 questions" with "Cross-examination topics and proposed questions (leave required)". Deadlines section: the 14-day notice, the hearing date, and for an Addendum the six-month window.
- A complaint follows Step 4A and Writing conventions 1, 2, 5 and 6. Convention 4 (s 139B and s 139E stated expressly) applies to registered practitioners only. For a social worker, each Conclusion names the code clause breached and the relief sought is what that body can give.
- **Release restriction comes before Annexure 1.** A party may not show a CIR or family report to anyone, including family and other professionals, without the Court's permission, and the court may restrict access (r 8.11(5)(d)); a copy the court gives out otherwise than by order must carry a notice saying who may have it (r 8.11(6)). Before quoting or extracting a report for any regulator or professional body, read the release order and the cover notice. If either restricts release, court permission is needed first. FLA s 114S(2)(d) answers the Part XIVB offence only; it does not override a court order. Until permission is given, the complaint cites paragraph and page numbers without quotation and asks the body to obtain the report from the Court.

## Writing conventions (mandatory for every output, review or complaint)

These are the user's standing requirements. Apply them to the review report, the complaint, annexures and any Word/PDF derived from them.

### 1. Explicit report references — never a bare number
Every reference to the expert's report says exactly what it points to.
- Front matter and unnumbered parts: "Section A, page 3", "Declaration, page 40". Never "p 3", "pp 2–3" or "page 3" alone when the part has a name.
- Numbered paragraphs: "paragraph 88.2", "At paragraph 41.5", "(paragraph 63)". Never "at 88.2", "(63)", "para 63" or "[63]".
- Ranges: "paragraphs 52 to 52.4", "paragraphs 70 to 72". Never an en dash range ("70–72") and never "paras".
- Headings follow the same rule: "Ground 2: Offered material not obtained (report paragraph 41.5)".
- Other documents get the same treatment: "transcript pages 10 to 14", "Affidavit of [date], page 5 (paragraph (c))", "Notice of Risk, page 4", "Appendix C, page 12".
- Before delivering, sweep the exported text with a regex for leftovers (`\bat \d`, `\(\d{1,3}(\.\d+)*\)`, `\bp \d`, `\bpp \d`, `\bparas?\b`, `\d{2,3}–\d{2,3}`) and fix every hit. Exclude ages like "[Child] (2)", statutory pinpoints like "s 214(1)(a)" and numbered instructions like "(1) Collect".

### 2. Ground structure — three steps, chronological
Each ground (or finding) is written as numbered Points, each Point in three parts:
1. **What the report says** — the bold lead line names the paragraph(s) ("**Point 2. What the report says at paragraph 88.2.**") followed by the report's own words in quotation marks.
2. ***What the expert had*** — an italic lead followed by a dated bullet list, strictly in date order, of the material the expert held or was offered (letters of instruction, enclosure lists, interview statements, agency records, court results), each bullet beginning with its date and ending with the appendix or annexure where it sits.
3. ***Conclusion.*** — one short paragraph stating the gap between 1 and 2 and naming the limb breached: (s 139B(1)(a)) for knowledge/skill/judgment/care, (s 139B(1)(l)) for candour/impropriety, plus the code clause (Medical Board of Australia, 2020, s. 10.9; RANZCP, 2020, s. 4.2.8).
Each ground opens with a one-paragraph intro, may close with "**Why it is a breach.**" where the limb needs argument, and always ends with "**Evidence.** Annexure 1 (…); Appendices …" listing every paragraph and appendix relied on. Events across a ground run chronologically; do not reorder for rhetorical effect.

### 3. Annexure 1 — verbatim report extracts
Never rely on the regulator having the report. Build "Annexure 1: Extracts from the report" reproducing word for word, under its paragraph number and page, every paragraph, Section A passage and Declaration passage cited anywhere in the body, in report order. Reference it from Part A, from the paragraph that explains why the report itself is not attached, from the Schedule row for the report, and from every ground's Evidence line. Where a court order bars annexing the report, say so and cite the order; rely on FLA s 114S(2)(d) (communication to a disciplinary body) for quoting it and naming the children.

### 4. Statutory breach stated expressly
A regulator will not infer the breach. State in the introduction to the grounds, in each ground's conclusion, and in the relief sought that the conduct is unsatisfactory professional conduct under s 139B(1)(a) and, where applicable, s 139B(1)(l) of the *Health Practitioner Regulation National Law (NSW)*, and that the instances together amount to professional misconduct under s 139E(b). Ask for assessment under s 23 of the *Health Care Complaints Act 1993* (NSW).

### 5. Names, citations and register
- Use the children's and parties' first names in the complaint and annexures (s 114S(2)(d) permits it); never initials. Pseudonymise only in material that could circulate publicly.
- Referencing is APA 7 with the AGLC4 hybrid for legislation: italic title including year, jurisdiction plain, no comma before the pinpoint, e.g. (*Family Law Act 1975* (Cth) s 60CC); codes cited author-date (Medical Board of Australia, 2020, s. 10.9; RANZCP, 2020, s. 4.2.2). Full reference list at the end.
- Plain, first-person, factual register. No em dashes anywhere in lodged text; minimal semicolons; no "not X but Y" pivots; no "genuinely", "honestly", "delve", "robust". Avoid "unlawfully arrested", "negligent", "vexatious", "false DV accusations", "clearly failed"; state what the record shows and let the regulator characterise it. Exact quotations only; redactions shown as ellipses; where a passage is transcribed from the user's notes, say so and ask the regulator to confirm it against the court's copy.
- Quote the opponent's or an agency's words only where the ground needs them, and never quote a passage the user has said is to be held on file only.

### 6. Deliverables and pipeline
- Master text lives in a Claude Docs document with a "Read this first" section, Parts A–G, References, Schedule of documents, a checklist and an Appendices table. The lodgement version strips "Read this first", the checklist and the Appendices table and retitles to "Complaint to the NSW Health Care Complaints Commission about the professional conduct of Dr [name]".
- Produce every time: LODGEMENT.docx (for the user to edit in Word), WORKING_DRAFT_with_notes.docx, LODGEMENT_BUNDLE.pdf (complaint + Annexure 1 + Appendices with index, cover sheets and bookmarks), and an annexures-only PDF. Redact third-party phone numbers and emails; a former address needs no redaction. Report word counts for the grounds and confirm zero em dashes before sending.
- Items not attached (the report itself, subpoena returns, Crown-copyright transcripts) appear in the Schedule as "NOT attached: Commission to obtain from the Court", with Advices of Court Result attached in place of a transcript.

## Workflow

### Step 0 — Gather inputs
Collect: (a) the SEW report; (b) letter of instructions and the appointing order; (c) agreed or separate statements of facts; (d) verifying affidavit; (e) fee correspondence; (f) any supplementary report or r 7.18(5) change-of-opinion notice; (g) the current parenting orders and the issues in dispute; (h) any s 67ZBD/67ZBE information sharing orders and the court's s 67ZBG decision on release of that material to the parties; (i) for a complaint, every enclosure list and transmission email showing exactly what the expert received and when. Items (b)–(i) are often missing: mark affected checks **Not assessable — request [document]** rather than guessing. Confirm from enclosure lists, not assumption, whether a document (for example a criminal transcript) ever reached the expert; frame the ground accordingly.

### Step 1 — Read the report fully
Extract: expert's name and qualifications; dates and mode of each assessment (in person / video / phone); who was seen, for how long, and with whom; collateral sources; documents read, including any police, child protection or firearms agency material and how it was obtained; psychometric tools; diagnoses; opinions; recommendations; affidavit (if attached); the child's age; any handwritten or marginal annotations; placeholders, blank paragraphs, truncated sentences and internal date or identity inconsistencies. Parse the report into numbered paragraphs with page numbers so Annexure 1 can be generated mechanically.

### Step 1A — Reconcile what the expert says was received
List every statement in the report about material: what was received, read, relied on, offered, declined, not provided or not available (for example "no school information was available"). Check each one against the enclosure lists, the schedule of documents sent, transmission emails and any acknowledgment of receipt. Record four things for each: the report's words with their paragraph, the record entry, the date, and whether they agree. A statement that material was missing or unavailable when the record shows it was sent or acknowledged is a finding under r 7.18 and Domain 4, graded at least Notable, and Critical where an opinion rests on the gap. Do this for every such statement, not only the ones the reviewing party raised. If no transmission record was supplied, mark the step **Not assessable — request [document]**.

### Step 2 — Run the checklist (five domains, below)
Score every item **Compliant / Partial / Non-compliant / Not assessable**. Every Partial or Non-compliant finding must carry a short quote from the report (explicit paragraph or section-and-page reference per Writing convention 1) and the exact rule, section or PPG clause.

### Step 3 — Grade severity
- **Critical** (serious weight or sanction exposure under r 7.23): no verifying affidavit or affidavit missing r 7.21(2) statements; opinion altered at a party's request / draft shown to a party; undisclosed conflict or treating role; no reasons or methodology (r 7.22); recommendations with no factual basis in the report; failure to consider facts detracting from the opinion (r 7.18(3)(d)); reliance on agency material the court had withheld from the parties under s 67ZBG or that reached the expert outside any court process; certifying complete enquiries while declining offered material.
- **Notable** (weight / challenge value): unstated limitations; expertise exceeded; opinion not delineated from fact; missing document list; gender-based or unsupported attachment assumptions; family violence not addressed where alleged; child's views reported without maturity qualification; agency material relied on without stating its source, date or whether it was complete; misattribution of a witness statement; selective use of a party-supplied bundle.
- **Minor**: style, verbosity, terminology (PPG3 §5.11; repealed section numbers), administrative defects, unproofed text (placeholders, blanks, truncated sentences, inconsistent dates).

### Step 4 — Produce both outputs (always)
1. **Chat checklist** — compact domain-by-domain table with scores and severity counts. Count each defect once. A defect that breaches several rules or appears under several domains is one finding: give it one number, list it under its main domain, and cross-refer to that number elsewhere. Severity totals count distinct findings, so the Critical, Notable and Minor totals add up to the number of numbered findings.
2. **Formal review report** — a Claude Docs document titled "SEW Compliance Review — [expert] ([report date])", sections: Executive summary → Inputs received / not assessable → Domain 1–5 findings tables (item, rule/clause, score, evidence quote, severity) → Overall rating (Compliant / Compliant with notable issues / Non-compliant) → Challenge strategy → Draft r 7.26 questions → Deadlines and information requests. Keep it private; do not share or publish.

### Step 4A — Regulator complaint (when asked)
Structure: Part A complainant and practitioner table (including why the report is not attached and the Annexure 1 reference) → Part B summary of grounds with the s 139B/s 139E statement → Part C background in numbered paragraphs → Part D grounds in the three-step structure → Part E why the matter is for the Commission despite settlement → Part F confidentiality and Part XIVB → Part G relief sought (findings under s 139B(1)(a)/(l) and s 139E(b)) → References → Schedule of documents → Annexure 1 → Appendices. Aim for the grounds to stay near 3,000 to 5,000 words; the three-step structure costs length, so cut background rather than evidence. **Conduct, not content:** the HCCC says it cannot assist with the content of a medico-legal or Family Court report and may take no further action under *Health Care Complaints Act 1993* (NSW) s 27(1)(c), but can consider the conduct of the practitioner who did the assessment. Frame every ground, and Part E, as conduct in carrying out the assessment and preparing the report, and say in Part B that the complaint does not ask the Commission to change any finding or order.

### Step 5 — Challenge strategy (label clearly as advocacy for the reviewing party)
For each Critical/Notable finding, list remedies in escalation order:
1. **r 7.26** written clarification questions — once only, within the rule's time limit (7 days after a conference, or 21 days after receipt if no conference — verify live); expert answers within the rule's period; expert need not answer until fees are secured (r 7.27).
2. **r 7.25** conference with the expert — within 21 days of receipt (verify live); parties' written agreement; clarification only, no interrogation.
3. **r 7.19** — the expert's own right to seek directions.
4. **r 7.08** permission to adduce another expert on the same issue — substantial body of contrary opinion, matters not known to the SEW, or other special reason (*Salmon & Salmon*).
5. **r 7.09** cross-examination — 14 days' written notice; cross-examining party pays attendance (verify live).
6. **r 7.23** sanctions — ask the court to refuse the report, reduce its weight, or reflect non-compliance in costs.
7. Weight submissions — expert's paramount duty and objectivity (r 7.18; PPG3 §4.1–4.3; PPG11 §4.2.11).
8. **Testing agency material** — if the expert relied on police or child protection material, consider asking the court to make or extend a s 67ZBD (particulars) or s 67ZBE (documents) order so the full record is before the court; where a s 67ZBE order has already issued to an agency, the party needs **leave under s 67ZBK** before subpoenaing that agency. Remind the lawyer that the court must admit relied-on Subdivision DA material (s 67ZBH) and that the *Family Law Regulations 2024* s 54 safeguards govern its use.
9. **Regulator complaint** — HCCC/Medical Council under the National Law, drafted per Step 4A, available after the proceedings settle or conclude.

### Step 6 — Draft r 7.26 questions
One or more per Critical/Notable finding: clarification only, non-vexatious, no unreasonable workload, each quoting the report's own text and asking for the basis, data, or limitation. Group by finding. Where agency material is involved, ask the expert to identify each document by agency, date and the order or process under which it was received. Flag the deadline and the r 7.27 fee point.

## Checklist

### Domain 1 — Formal requirements (rr 7.21, 7.22)
- Addressed to the court and the instructing party/parties (r 7.21(1)(a))
- Summary of instructions attached (r 7.21(1)(b))
- List of documents relied on attached (r 7.21(1)(b))
- Verified by the expert's affidavit (r 7.21(1)(c)); an unsworn "Declaration" is not an affidavit
- Affidavit states: Rules read and understood; best endeavours to comply; all necessary inquiries made; opinions independent, impartial and factual; duty to the court understood and complied with; professional code complied with (r 7.21(2); PPG3 §5.5.3)
- Reasons for conclusions (r 7.22(1)(a)); methodology (r 7.22(1)(b))
- Qualifications included and accurately presented (r 7.22(2)(a); PPG11 §4.2.6)
- Literature/material relied on (r 7.22(2)(b))
- Facts, matters and assumptions underlying each opinion (r 7.22(2)(c))
- Document proofed: no placeholders, blank paragraphs, truncated sentences or internal date/identity contradictions. No rule requires proofing as such: raise these as going to the reliability of the verification (r 7.21(1)(c), r 7.21(2)) and of the contents (r 7.22), not as a breach of those rules

### Domain 2 — Appointment and instructions (rr 7.03–7.06, 7.13; PPG3 §5.1, §5.12)
- Valid appointment: joint agreement (r 7.03) or court order with expert's consent (r 7.04)
- Instructions in writing (r 7.13(3); PPG3 §5.1), including: request for a written report; advice the report may be used in proceedings; the issues; matters to investigate; full and frank disclosure of relevant information
- Agreed statement of facts, or separate statements with leave (r 7.13(4)–(5))
- Expert given and read Divisions 7.1.4–7.1.6 (r 7.13(2)(a))
- Specific questions posed (PPG3 §5.1); opinions confined to those questions
- Fees: shares agreed, fee letter exists (r 7.06; PPG3 §5.12); no outcome-contingent fee (PPG11 §4.1.3)

### Domain 3 — Duty to the court, bias, objectivity (r 7.18; PPG3 §4; PPG11 §4.2.11, §6)
- Objective, unbiased, independent, impartial (r 7.18(3)(a))
- All material facts considered, including facts that detract from the opinion (r 7.18(3)(d))
- Out-of-expertise issues flagged and not then opined on (r 7.18(3)(e)(i); PPG11 §4.2.5)
- Incompleteness or inaccurate/incomplete research flagged (r 7.18(3)(e)(ii))
- Not an advocate (PPG3 §4.3)
- Opinion delineated from fact; reasoning explained (PPG11 §4.2.8)
- No draft shown; opinion not altered at a party's request (PPG11 §4.2.11). A change of opinion after the report is given must be notified in writing (r 7.18(5): to the instructing party, or for a court-appointed expert to the Registry Manager and each party); the rule says "written notice", not "supplementary report", so cite PPG11 for the supplementary report point
- No disparaging comment on other professionals (PPG11 §6.3)
- No conflict of interest; treating role disclosed (PPG11 §4.3, §6.4; r 7.01(1)(a))
- Offered or available material (full subpoena return, treating practitioner, later supervision reports) obtained before certifying that all desirable enquiries were made

### Domain 4 — Assessment conduct and clinical quality (PPG11 §4.2, §5; PPG3 §5.4)
- Request source and all information sources disclosed (PPG11 §4.2.1–4.2.2)
- Purpose, context and limits of confidentiality explained to each person examined (PPG11 §5.1.1); capacity to consent considered (§5.1.2)
- Support persons disclosed with impact (PPG11 §5.2.3)
- Video/phone mode and technical issues documented (PPG11 §5.2.7)
- Surveillance material handled with natural justice (PPG11 §5.2.5)
- Evaluation as comprehensive as possible; limits acknowledged (PPG3 §5.4.2); each party's understanding ascertained (PPG3 §5.4.3)
- Limitations and reasonable medical certainty expressed; desktop limits acknowledged (PPG11 §4.2.4)
- Degree of certitude; grounded in clinical experience and peer-reviewed literature (PPG11 §4.2.10)
- No-diagnosis clearly stated where data supports none (PPG11 §4.2.9); personality-disorder language not used where the expert concedes longitudinal assessment is needed
- Further useful information identified (PPG11 §4.2.3)
- Respectful language (PPG11 §6.2)
- Psychometric instruments named, validated for purpose, scores interpreted with norms and caveats

#### Domain 4A — Agency material and information sharing (FLA Subdiv DA ss 67ZBC–67ZBK; Family Law Regulations 2024 s 54; Part XIVB)
- Each item of police, child protection or firearms agency material is identified by agency, date and description
- The route by which it reached the expert is stated: court order under s 67ZBD (particulars) or s 67ZBE (documents/information), agency's own initiative (ss 67ZBD(5)/67ZBE(5)), subpoena, or supplied by a party; material supplied by a party only is flagged for completeness and selection bias
- Expert has not relied on material the court withheld from the parties under s 67ZBG, or on protected material an agency was not required to provide (s 67ZBF)
- Expert distinguishes allegation, investigation outcome and finding in agency records, and notes any correction or update notified under the *Family Law Regulations 2024* s 54 safeguards
- Witness statements in agency records attributed to the correct person; where an allegation exists in several versions, all are disclosed and the version adopted is explained
- Expert states whether the agency record appeared complete and whether further particulars (s 67ZBD) would assist (PPG11 §4.2.3)
- Material from other proceedings handled consistently with Part XIVB; criminal outcomes (acquittal, ADVO refusal, costs orders) stated accurately and not undercut by a party's contrary account

### Domain 5 — Child welfare and recommendations (FLA s 60CC, s 60CG; PPG3 §2)
- Recommendations serve the child's best interests, not a party's (PPG3 §2.1)
- Each current s 60CC(2) consideration addressed where relevant: safety (including family violence history and orders); child's views; developmental, psychological, emotional and cultural needs; each person's capacity to meet those needs; benefit of relationships with parents and significant others; any other relevant circumstance. s 60CC(3) culture for an Aboriginal or Torres Strait Islander child.
- Safety findings reconciled with the agency material in Domain 4A; any divergence explained
- Attachment reasoning evidence-based; safe/secure attachment prioritised (PPG3 §2.2.1)
- Sibling unity considered (PPG3 §2.2.3)
- No gender-based custody preference; infancy exception reasoned if invoked (PPG3 §2.2.4, §2.2.4.1)
- Child's views conveyed with maturity/capacity qualifications (PPG3 §2.2.6)
- Impact of delay on attachment addressed (PPG3 §2.2.5)
- Each recommendation traced to assessment data in the body; flag any with no supporting evidence

## Rules of engagement

- Never assert a finding without quoting the report (explicit reference per Writing convention 1) and citing the exact rule, section or PPG clause.
- Check every pinpoint before delivering. For each report reference in the output, look the paragraph number up in the parsed report from Step 1 and confirm that the section letter and the page number are the ones that paragraph actually sits in. Do not carry a section or page over from a neighbouring reference. Where one reference covers paragraphs in different sections or on different pages, give each its own section and page. Fix every mismatch; a wrong pinpoint in a complaint is a defect of the reviewer's own.
- Cite only what this skill names or what you have opened and read at its source in this session. Do not add a case name, medium neutral citation, section or rule number from memory. Where this skill names a case without a citation, use the case name exactly as this skill writes it and mark it "citation to be verified". You may add a citation found by search only if you opened the judgment or an official page for it and you give all four of these, copied from that page: the link, the court, the judge or judges, and the decision date. If any one is missing, or two sources give different citations, give the name only, mark it "citation to be verified" and say what the sources disagree on. A label such as "found by search" does not replace these four items.
- Mark anything unverifiable as **Not assessable — request [document]** and carry it into the information-request list and r 7.26 questions.
- Flag any sign the expert treated the child or a party (PPG11 §4.3; r 7.01(1)(a)).
- Compliance audit only — no advice on the merits of the parenting dispute. Recommend the reviewing party's lawyer confirm strategy and deadlines.
- **Confidentiality (FLA Part XIVB, ss 114N–114S).** It is an offence to communicate to the public an account of proceedings that identifies a party, child, witness or other person involved. A communication to a person with a significant and legitimate interest in the matter (the reviewing party, their lawyer or counsel) or to a disciplinary body (s 114S(2)(d)) is not "to the public", so the review report and a regulator complaint may go to them with real names. Beyond that: keep all outputs private, never publish or share an artifact, use initials or pseudonyms for parties and children in any document that might circulate, and warn the user if they ask to post, publish or forward the review more widely. Agency material obtained under Subdivision DA carries the *Family Law Regulations 2024* s 54 use safeguards as well. **A court order comes first:** if the order releasing a report (or its cover notice) limits who may have it, s 114S(2)(d) does not permit sending, quoting or extracting it to a regulator. That needs the Court's permission. This overrides Writing convention 3 until permission is given or the user's lawyer advises otherwise. Do not attach privileged lawyer–client correspondence; do not attach a transcript carrying a Crown copyright notice, attach the Advice of Court Result instead.
