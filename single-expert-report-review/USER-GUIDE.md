# User guide: Single Expert and Court Report Review

**Skill folder:** `single-expert-report-review`

## What it does
Audits an expert or court report in FCFCOA parenting proceedings. It covers two kinds of report, because different rules bind each author:

- **Single expert reports** by a psychiatrist, psychologist or social worker, checked against Part 7.1 of the Family Law Rules 2021, the Family Law Act 1975 and the author's professional standards.
- **Court reports** by a Court Child Expert or Panel Family Consultant: a Child Impact Report, Specific Issues Report, Family Report or an addendum or update, checked against section 62G, rule 8.11, the order and the Australian Standards of Practice.

It scores each requirement, grades severity, suggests how to challenge the report, and can draft a conduct complaint.

## When to use it
Use it when you receive a report you believe is biased, incomplete or unsupported, while there is still time to act in the case, or after the case ends if you want to complain about the author's conduct.

**Example requests:**
- "Review this single expert report for compliance with the Rules."
- "Check this Child Impact Report against the order and the Family Law Act."
- "Draft the questions I can send the expert under rule 7.26."
- "Turn the findings into a conduct complaint."

## What to give it
Upload or paste these. The first one is required; the rest make the result stronger.

1. **The report** (required).
2. The order that appointed the author or directed the report, and any letter of instructions. For a court report, the order names the issues the report must address.
3. The verifying affidavit, any supplementary report, and fee correspondence.
4. The current orders and the issues in dispute.
5. For a complaint: every list of documents and email showing what the expert received and when.

If something is missing, the skill marks the affected checks "Not assessable" and asks for it. It asks one question at a time.

## What you get back
- A chat checklist: domain-by-domain scores (Compliant / Partial / Non-compliant / Not assessable) and severity counts.
- A formal review report: summary, findings tables with quotes, overall rating, challenge strategy, draft rule 7.26 questions, and deadlines.
- On request: a conduct complaint to the right body for the author's profession, with grounds, extracts and a schedule of documents, as Word and PDF files.

## How to read the results
- **Critical**: could seriously reduce the report's weight or lead to sanctions (for example no reasons, undisclosed conflict, ignoring facts against the opinion).
- **Notable**: useful in challenging the report.
- **Minor**: style or administrative issues.

Defects usually go to **weight**, not admissibility. The skill will not call a report "inadmissible".

## Tips
- **Act fast.** For a single expert, rule 7.25 and 7.26 time limits are short (often 7 to 21 days). For a court report, cross-examining the author needs written notice (usually 14 days before the hearing).
- You can only send rule 7.26 questions once, so use the drafted set carefully. They do not apply to a court report writer; there the skill drafts cross-examination topics instead.
- **Complain about conduct, not content.** Regulators will not change a finding. Each ground must be something the author did or failed to do.
- **Check the release order first.** You may need the Court's permission before quoting a court report to a regulator or professional body. The skill checks this before it extracts anything.
- A complaint is usually made after the case settles or ends. The Court will not look into a complaint about its own report writer while the case is running.

## Before you rely on it
- **Not legal advice.** It checks documents against the law. It does not tell you what to do in your case. Speak to a solicitor, Legal Aid or LawAccess NSW before acting.
- **Check every citation.** The skill tells the AI to verify current law, but AI can still be wrong. Open each section and case it cites.
- **Keep it private.** Section 114Q of the Family Law Act 1975 (Cth) makes it an offence to publish anything that identifies people in family law proceedings. Never post a report online or share it outside your lawyer, the court or a regulator.
