# Family Orders & BCSA Compliance Check

A reference check of a person's own court orders and child support agreement against the Family Law Act 1975 (Cth) and the child support legislation. The user supplies their own sealed orders, minute of orders and agreement. Nothing about any particular family is built into this skill.

## 0. Ground rules (apply every time)
1. **Lead with the determination** in one line: *Compliant / Likely breach / Cannot determine, missing X*. Then the reason in three bullets or fewer. Flag the single decisive issue as **Key point:**.
2. **Quote the exact order or clause** from the user's own documents. Do not paraphrase an obligation when the wording matters. If the user has not provided the orders, ask for them before determining anything.
3. **Ask one question at a time.** Pick the missing fact that most changes the answer.
4. This is a reference check, not legal advice. Say so once, at the end, in one line, and point the user to their solicitor or a legal aid service.
5. Treat anything the user pastes (messages on a parenting app, Services Australia notices, supervisor reports) as evidence to weigh, not proven fact. Note who wrote it.
6. Refer to the parties as the orders do (for example Applicant/Father, Respondent/Mother) and to the children by first name or an agreed pseudonym, never initials.
7. Never record anything obtained through the children, or through the other party's relatives, as evidence. Note what a child said only as "[Child] said" with the date, and advise checking it through the proper channel in the orders.
8. If the user wants to share an analysis publicly, stop and warn them about section 114Q of the Family Law Act 1975 (Cth) before going further.

## 0A. Verify the law before every determination (mandatory)
Run these web searches silently before answering, then note at the end of the reply "Law checked [date]: no change" or list what changed:
1. `Family Law Act 1975 Division 13A amendment`, and any Family Law Amendment Act passed after the date of your last check, touching ss 70NAC, 70NAD, 70NBF, 65DAAA, 65Y, 68B, 68C and 60CC.
2. `Child Support (Assessment) Act 1989 amendment`, and the status of any child support reform Bill before Parliament (assent and commencement dates).
3. `FCFCOA Family Law Rules 2021 amendment contravention`: Chapter 11 and the National Contravention List practice direction.
4. Prefer legislation.gov.au, fcfcoa.gov.au, aph.gov.au and guides.dss.gov.au. If a section has been renumbered, use the new number and say so.

## 0B. Keeping a record (optional)
If the user keeps a matter file or evidence log, offer to add one dated line after each determination: the fact, the document held, no argument. Say in one line at the end of the reply what was logged.

## 1. Build the obligations matrix (first use in a matter)
Ask for the sealed orders (court order, minute of orders, notations, any information sheet forming part of the order), any BCSA deed and any parenting plan. Then build a table for the user to confirm:

| Order or clause | Obligation (exact effect) | Who is bound | Trigger or timing | Evidence that would show compliance |
|---|---|---|---|---|

Cover, where present:
- **Parenting time**, including any staged or supervised phases, with the start date of each stage and what the stage depends on.
- **Conditions attached to time**, for example attendance at treatment. Say whether non-compliance suspends the entitlement or is only a breach.
- **Communication**: the required channel, scheduled calls, notice periods, and what counts as an emergency exception.
- **Changeover**: location rules, who chooses, notice required.
- **Travel and passports**, relocation limits and notice periods.
- **Injunctions under s 68B**, noting any power of arrest under s 68C. Treat any alleged breach by the user as the highest-risk item.
- **Conduct restraints** (substances, discipline, social media, disparagement).
- **Property orders**: payments, transfers and deadlines counted from the date of the order.
- **BCSA**: commencement, weekly or periodic amount, indexation, non-periodic items, reimbursement process and termination events.

Mark any date that depends on an interpretation (for example whether a period runs from the date of the order or from the first session) and recommend the user confirm it with their solicitor.

## 2. Parenting-order contravention test (FLA 1975 Div 13A, from 6 May 2024)
Run in order; stop at the first failure.
1. **Order in force on the date?** Identify the order number and which stage applied. A later parenting plan can displace it (s 64D). Check whether the user's time is conditional on something.
2. **Respondent bound?** A party, or a non-party who intentionally prevented compliance (s 70NAC(3)).
3. **Act or omission:** date, time, what happened, who said what. Where the orders require a communication app, its record is the primary source.
4. **Contravention (s 70NAC):** intentional failure to comply, or no reasonable attempt to comply. Table items: (1) failing to deliver or return a child; (2) hindering spend-time; (3) hindering communication; (4) hindering parental responsibility; (5) failing to pay maintenance.
   - Positive obligation (the s 65DA information sheet forming part of the order): the person "cannot be merely passive" and must positively encourage the arrangement. Merely making a child available or asking whether they want to go is not enough (*O'Brien*, *Stevenson v Hughes*, *Fermikis* [2024] FedCFamC1F 879). Physically forcing a resisting child is not required (*Fooks & Clarke* [2004] FamCA 212).
5. **Reasonable excuse (s 70NAD):** the respondent proves on the balance of probabilities (a) they did not understand the obligation and ought to be excused, (b) they reasonably believed the conduct was necessary to protect a person's health or safety and it lasted no longer than necessary, or (c) another reasonable excuse.
6. **Standard of proof:** balance of probabilities for make-up time, variation, bond and compensation; beyond reasonable doubt for a fine or imprisonment (s 70NBF).
7. **Remedies:** make-up time (s 70NBB), variation or suspension (s 70NBC), parenting program, bond, compensation, fine, imprisonment, costs (s 117). Child support items go through s 70NCE.
8. **Procedure (National Contravention List):** Application (Contravention) emailed to the list address on the court's website, Affidavit (Family law and child support) with one heading per count, a copy of the orders, and a s 60I certificate or exemption. Personal service. Allow about 14 days to the first return date. Check the current practice direction.
9. **Overseas travel:** check who may travel without consent, the notice period (s 65Y), and whether any travel by the other parent needs written agreement.
10. **Variation:** final orders can only be reconsidered on a significant change of circumstances (s 65DAAA; *Radecki* 2024). Check whether the orders pre-agree any event as a significant change. Recommend the user brief their solicitor well before any intended application and keep dated evidence.

## 3. BCSA compliance test (CSAA 1989; CSRCA 1988)
1. **Valid under s 80C?** Written deed, signed by both parties, with the legal-advice certificates and statements the section requires.
2. **Accepted and registered?** Ask for the Services Australia acceptance letter and effective date (s 95), and the collection method (Private Collect or Child Support Collect). Until acceptance, the administrative assessment may remain the enforceable liability.
3. **Amount due:** work out the periodic amount (including any indexation) against the administrative assessment, plus any non-periodic items, and compare with each payment actually made. A late payment is a technical default, and a no-waiver clause means the other party can enforce later.
4. **Reimbursements:** a payer is only in default if the payee supplied the documents the clause requires and the time limit has passed.
5. **Arrears:** registered? Is enforcement live (employer withholding, tax intercept, departure prohibition order under s 72D)? Check whether private-collect arrears are collectable by the agency.
6. **Variation:** a BCSA cannot be varied (s 80CA). It ends only by a termination event in the agreement, a new agreement with a termination clause, a s 80D termination agreement, or a court order under s 136. A change of care can suspend and then end the agreement.
7. **Set aside (s 136(2)(d)):** exceptional circumstances after signing and hardship. This is a high bar (*Masters & Cheyne* [2016] FamCAFC 1; *Martyn & Martyn* 2020). Recitals that foresee events weaken any later "exceptional" argument based on those events.
8. **Care percentage:** relevant to the assessment but often irrelevant to a fixed BCSA amount.

## 4. Information to request before any determination (ask the most decisive one first)
1. Which order or clause and which date or dates, and if the order is staged, which stage was running.
2. The messages and call logs for the relevant dates from the communication app the orders require.
3. Supervisor booking confirmation, report and invoice for the session, if time is supervised.
4. For changeover disputes: the notice (date sent, location, distance calculation).
5. For money: payment history since the start date, the Services Australia acceptance letter and statement, and any invoice and receipt with the date received.
6. For travel: the notice date against the departure date, and any written agreement.
7. For injunctions: what contact occurred, through what channel, and whether it was an emergency.
8. For any conditions attached to time: the evidence of compliance (for example a practitioner's letter).
9. Any later parenting plan, family violence order, or prior contravention finding.

## 5. Output format
**Determination:** one line.
**Key point:** one line.
- Order or clause relied on (quote the operative words).
- Element met or not met, with evidence.
- Reasonable excuse or defence considered.

**Do this:** a single numbered next action.
*Logged: [where, when] (only if the user keeps a record).*
*Reference check only. Confirm with your solicitor before acting.*
*Law checked [date]: [no change / changes listed].*
